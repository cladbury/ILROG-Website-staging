Ongoing Research

ILROG member institutions run prospective trials and multicenter studies on radiation dose, technique and outcomes.

Collaborative Studies Open to ILROG Centers

Retrospective registry

Secondary CNS lymphoma and myeloma

Enrolling centers

Usage of Radiotherapy for Secondary Central Nervous System Hematologic Malignancies: A Registry of the International Lymphoma Radiation Oncology Group

Up to 5% of patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma develop CNS involvement, and current radiotherapy recommendations in this setting are drawn largely from the primary CNS lymphoma literature. This registry collects patients who received CNS-directed radiotherapy for synchronous or metachronous CNS involvement by follicular, marginal zone, mantle cell, diffuse large B-cell, transformed, high-grade B-cell or peripheral T-cell lymphoma, or multiple myeloma. Parenchymal, vitreoretinal, leptomeningeal and dural disease are included; bony disease extending into the CNS is not. The primary objective is CNS progression-free survival. Secondary objectives describe the indications for radiotherapy, dose and fractionation, target volume (focal, whole brain or craniospinal), timing relative to CAR T-cell therapy and transplant, and toxicity. A companion question from Duke compares focal radiotherapy with whole-brain radiotherapy. The target is approximately 200 patients.

Principal Investigators
Dr Kathryn Tringale (UC San Diego) and Dr Brandon Imber (Memorial Sloan Kettering)
Study Team
Gustav Cederquist, Joanna Yang, Harper Hubbeling, Nicholas Figura, Austin Sim, Christopher Kelsey and Eugene Vaios (associate investigators); Jiwan Kohli (statistician)

Protocol version 2, November 2025. Centers interested in contributing patients can contact Dr Tringale through ILROG.

Retrospective, multicenter

Primary CNS lymphoma

Seeking centers

Involved-Site Radiotherapy for Consolidation or Salvage Treatment in Primary Central Nervous System Lymphoma

High-dose methotrexate is the backbone of treatment for primary CNS lymphoma, but fewer than half of patients achieve remission. Whole-brain radiotherapy is effective but causes neurocognitive impairment, so many centers now use focal, involved-site radiotherapy as consolidation, salvage or bridging. A RedCap database built by the German Society for Radiation Oncology (DEGRO) and the German Lymphoma Alliance is already collecting these patients in Germany, and this study opens it to ILROG centers. Eligible patients have aggressive lymphoma with primary CNS involvement treated with localized (non-whole-brain) radiotherapy between 2010 and 2024. Participating sites need joint involvement of their radiation oncology and hematology departments. The primary endpoints are duration of locoregional control, separating in-field from out-of-field CNS recurrence, and toxicity graded by CTCAE version 5.0. A move to prospective data collection will be discussed later.

Principal Investigators
Dr Niklas Benedikt Pepper, Dr Michael Oertel and Prof Hans Theodor Eich (University Hospital of Münster)

Proposal dated March 2026. Contact ILROG to join.

Model development and validation

Hodgkin lymphoma with mediastinal disease

Seeking contributors

Estimating Risk of Radiation Toxicity for Lymphoma Patients Using Pre-Chemotherapy PET-CT Scans

Radiotherapy risk is usually estimated only after chemotherapy, once a planning CT and treatment plan exist. This study aims to estimate the dose to the heart, lungs and breasts from the pre-chemotherapy PET-CT, so that the expected risk of late effects can be weighed when the overall treatment approach is first decided. A pilot study of 169 patients with mediastinal Hodgkin lymphoma from 9 UK and European centers produced a regression model predicting mean heart dose from disease location and extent on PET-CT, delivery technique and breathing mode. The expanded study will collect data from more centers to refine and validate the model and extend it to cardiac substructures, lung and breast. Each case needs measurements from the pre-chemotherapy PET-CT, the radiotherapy fields and technique, and organ-at-risk doses from the treatment plan; a study atlas and data collection spreadsheet are provided.

Principal Investigators
Dr Rebecca Shakir, Dr Lorna Roden and Dr David Cutter (Oxford Population Health, University of Oxford)

Funded by Cancer Research UK. Presented at the ILROG research meeting, October 2024.

Retrospective, multicenter

Hematologic malignancies treated with CAR T-cells or bispecific antibodies

Data collection

Multicenter Review of Radiation During Peri-Treatment With Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-Cell Therapy and/or Bispecific Antibodies for Hematologic Malignancies

This study builds on the ILROG bridging radiotherapy study below. It reviews patients who underwent apheresis for commercial CAR T-cell therapy, or who received bispecific antibodies, between 2018 and 2023, with and without radiotherapy. The aims are to describe how radiotherapy is combined with these therapies across centers, and to compare cytokine release syndrome, neurotoxicity, response and survival in patients treated with and without radiotherapy. Penn leads the analysis using limited data sets and de-identified imaging from each center.

Principal Investigators
Dr John Plastaras (University of Pennsylvania)

Data are shared under collaborative research agreements with the University of Pennsylvania.

Retrospective, multicenter

Indolent orbital adnexal lymphoma

Accrual ongoing

Orbital Adnexal Indolent Lymphoma: A Retrospective Study of the International Lymphoma Radiation Oncology Group (4 Gy Extension Study)

This study collects patients with marginal zone, follicular, small lymphocytic or low-grade lymphoma of the orbital adnexa treated with 4 Gy in 1 or 2 fractions to at least one orbit, with palliative or definitive intent, between 1980 and 2021.

Principal Investigators
Dr Andrew Wirth (Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre), Dr Chelsea Pinnix (MD Anderson), Dr Brandon Imber (Memorial Sloan Kettering) and Dr Michael Binkley (Stanford)
Study Team
Lilly Shen (associate investigator); Mathias Bressel (statistician)

As of March 2024, 172 patients had been entered from 17 centers, with 3 more centers pending.
